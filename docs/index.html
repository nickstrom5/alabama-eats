Built around how people here eat

Star ratings won't tell you whose chicken still goes into the white sauce straight off the pit, or which fish camp the state put on its Catfish Trail. Alabama Eats is organized the way people here eat: white sauce and barbecue, meat-and-threes, Gulf oysters, catfish houses and Greek diners and hot dogs, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source. Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out on a back road.

What stays on your phone

Alabama Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Alabama Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Two kinds of source. The barbecue, meat-and-three, oyster, catfish, Greek diner and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a 2025 or 2026 source behind each place: the Alabama Tourism Department's Catfish Trail, the Gulf Shores & Orange Beach and Mobile tourism boards, the MICHELIN Guide or a dated local story. The wider directory of 10,274 restaurants starts from open map listings (Overture Maps), filtered to the kinds that matched an inspected business most often in Jefferson and Montgomery counties.

Where did Alabama white sauce start?

In Decatur: Bob Gibson began smoking chickens in his backyard in 1925, and Big Bob Gibson Bar-B-Q still dips them in its mayonnaise-and-vinegar white sauce straight off the pit. Our barbecue list has 9 hand-checked places, from Decatur to Mobile.

How do I check an Alabama restaurant's inspection score?

Alabama's county health departments score restaurants on a 100-point scale (85 and up is satisfactory). Montgomery County and the Jefferson County Department of Health publish each place's latest score as a list, and the app shows that score on the place, dated and labeled as the county's; it computes no grade. Elsewhere the scores are in the Alabama Department of Public Health's search, which each place links to.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

No. Alabama's ABC Board publishes no licensee list, so the app doesn't say either way.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
